Knoxville or Asheville for retirement?

Two Southern Appalachian cities about 115 miles apart on I-40, sharing the same mountains and the same weak airports, priced $87,000 apart. Here is the honest tradeoff.

The short version

Choose Knoxville if money, medical depth and tax burden are what decide this, and Asheville if trail access, a walkable downtown and a real arts scene are worth $87,000 more on the house and a full budget tier. The catch they share: neither has a strong airport, and Helene ran through both of these valleys in 2024.

What they share: the same mountains, and the same airport problem

These are neighbors. About 115 miles of I-40 separate them, roughly a two-hour drive, and they share a climate: four real seasons, humid summers, and mountains in every direction. They also share a weakness neither can fix. Airport access scores 6 of 10 to Asheville's 5, which is Knoxville edging a comparison rather than winning one. McGhee Tyson publishes more than thirty nonstop destinations and gained Southwest service in March 2026; Asheville Regional publishes twenty-six across five airlines. Both sit about twenty minutes from downtown, and from either one most of the world is a connection through Atlanta or Charlotte.

Money: Knoxville is cheaper by a full budget tier

A typical Knoxville home runs $377,000 against Asheville's $464,000, and the monthly retiree estimate is $500 lower at both ends of the range. That is a whole budget tier, not a rounding difference. A retiree selling into either market can buy in Knoxville with more left over, and the gap keeps working: property tax runs 0.52% against 0.66%, on a smaller purchase price. One thing not to double-count, because it is easy to: the insurance estimates, $2,958 and $3,124 a year, already sit inside those monthly figures. The insurance advantage is part of the $500, not another saving on top of it.

Healthcare: Knoxville's widest margin, for a reason worth understanding

Healthcare scores 8 of 10 to Asheville's 5, and that gap is about depth of options rather than the quality of any one hospital. Mission Hospital is a genuinely good hospital: US News rates it seventh in North Carolina and high performing in seventeen adult procedures. But Asheville is a one-system town, and that system has been strained since HCA acquired it, rating two of five stars for patient experience on Medicare's Hospital Compare and drawing a CMS immediate jeopardy finding in February 2024 that was later lifted. Knoxville gives you somewhere else to go: the University of Tennessee Medical Center ranks first in the metro and fifth in the state, with Covenant Health Parkwest second in the metro and eighth in the state. If you want a second opinion in Asheville, you drive for it.

Each city's signature: Asheville's mountains, Knoxville's math

Asheville's outdoor recreation scores 10 of 10 to Knoxville's 8, with the Blue Ridge Parkway at the edge of town and Pisgah National Forest immediately south. Sitting above 2,000 feet also buys it summers people actually want, a 4 of 10 on heat severity against Knoxville's 7. And it is the more urban of the two on foot: walkability scores 7 of 10 to Knoxville's 5, and community scores 9 of 10 to Knoxville's 8.

Knoxville's case is that it has no soft spot. The Great Smokies are under an hour east. Safety scores 7 of 10 to Asheville's 5. And tax friendliness scores 9 of 10 to Asheville's 6, which is real money rather than a rating: Tennessee has no state income tax, so pensions and IRA and 401(k) withdrawals go untaxed by the state, while North Carolina exempts Social Security and taxes the rest at a flat 3.99% for 2026.

Risk: Helene ran through both of these valleys

Asheville's disaster resilience scores 5 of 10 to Knoxville's 6, and the honest reading of that is a difference of degree. Helene's 2024 inland flooding was catastrophic around Asheville and ended the idea that mountains at this latitude are a hedge against weather; Knoxville sits on the edge of the same region, and flooding is named in its own risk profile. Neither city carries coastal insurance pricing. Both deserve a look at the specific property's flood maps before you sign anything, particularly near the French Broad or the Tennessee River.

Frequently asked

Is Knoxville or Asheville better for retirement?

Knoxville is the stronger pick on money, medical depth and taxes, while Asheville wins on outdoor access, a walkable downtown and arts. Knoxville's healthcare scores 8 of 10 to Asheville's 5 and its tax friendliness scores 9 of 10 to Asheville's 6, and it costs about $87,000 less on a typical home. Asheville's outdoor recreation scores 10 of 10 to Knoxville's 8, and its walkability scores 7 of 10 to Knoxville's 5.

Which is cheaper, Knoxville or Asheville?

Knoxville, by a full budget tier. It sits in tier 1 with a typical home value around $377,000 and a retiree budget of about $4,900 to $6,200 a month. Asheville sits in tier 2 at roughly $464,000 and $5,400 to $6,700 a month, with a higher property tax rate as well (0.66% against 0.52%).

Which has better healthcare, Knoxville or Asheville?

Knoxville, and the margin is wide: healthcare scores 8 of 10 to Asheville's 5. The reason is depth rather than quality. Knoxville has two separately ranked hospital systems in the metro, the University of Tennessee Medical Center and Covenant Health Parkwest. Asheville is effectively a one-system town built around Mission Hospital, which posts strong clinical outcomes but rates two of five stars for patient experience on Medicare's Hospital Compare and was placed under a CMS immediate jeopardy finding in February 2024, since lifted.

Which has lower taxes for retirees, Knoxville or Asheville?

Knoxville. Tennessee levies no state income tax at all, so Social Security, pensions, IRA and 401(k) withdrawals are untaxed by the state. North Carolina exempts Social Security but taxes other retirement income at a flat 3.99% for 2026. Knoxville's property tax rate is lower too, 0.52% against 0.66%, which is why tax friendliness scores 9 of 10 to Asheville's 6.

Is Asheville still worth retiring to after Hurricane Helene, compared with Knoxville?

Helene caused catastrophic inland flooding around Asheville in 2024 and ended its billing as a climate haven. Asheville's disaster resilience scores 5 of 10 to Knoxville's 6, so this is a difference of degree, not of kind: Knoxville sits on the edge of the same region and its own database note names flooding as a risk. Check a specific property's flood maps in either city, particularly near the French Broad or the Tennessee River.

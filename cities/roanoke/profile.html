Should you actually move here?

Is Roanoke for you?

Roanoke is a value pick with a specific shape: mountains you can reach from a downtown sidewalk, a cost of living about 8 percent below the national average, and a real regional hospital, wrapped in a small city that keeps a genuine arts and dining scene alive. The retirees who thrive here want the Blue Ridge more than a big airport, and they are fine trading a metropolis for a valley. Some fall hard for it. Others miss the nonstop flights and the bigger cultural menu.

✓
You'll love it here if…
  • The mountains are the point. McAfee Knob, the most photographed viewpoint on the Appalachian Trail, is a 15-minute drive and a moderate hike from downtown. Carvins Cove, the nation's second-largest municipal park, holds more than 60 miles of trail eight miles out, and the Blue Ridge Parkway runs the ridgelines above town.
  • You want a real city's amenities at a small-city price. A typical home value near $285K, a walkable downtown market, the free Taubman Museum of Art, a symphony and a restored 1920s theatre in Grandin Village. The dollar goes a long way here without feeling like a compromise.
  • Regional healthcare depth reassures you. Carilion Roanoke Memorial is the region's only Level I trauma center and its Best Regional Hospital in Southwestern Virginia, rated high performing in 15 procedures and conditions. It is the teaching hospital for the Virginia Tech Carilion School of Medicine, so specialist depth runs deeper than the population would suggest.
  • You want four real seasons with a gentle winter. January averages in the mid-30s and snow runs light, around 15 inches a year, but autumn color on the ridges is genuine and spring comes early. Seasons without the shoveling of the true north.
✗
Skip Roanoke if…
  • You fly often and need a hub from your doorstep. Roanoke-Blacksburg Regional runs about 10 nonstop destinations across four carriers, mostly hub connections like Charlotte, Atlanta, Chicago, and a new daily Dallas-Fort Worth flight added in June 2026. Handy, but most trips still route through a hub rather than fly direct.
  • You want to live car-free. Downtown and Grandin Village are genuinely walkable, and the greenway ties them together, but the rest of the valley needs a car for daily errands.
  • No state income tax is a must. Virginia taxes pension, IRA, and 401(k) income up to 5.75 percent. Social Security is exempt and filers 65 and older get an age deduction of up to $12,000, but this is not a no-tax state like Tennessee next door.
  • You want a big-city cultural menu or pro sports. The arts and dining scene is real and growing, but it is small-city in scale. There are no major-league teams, and the deepest cultural calendars are a few hours away in larger metros.
The character of the place

A mountain town that grew into a real city.

Roanoke exists because of the railroad. In the 1880s the Norfolk and Western made this valley its hub, and a village of a few hundred became a working city almost overnight. The trains still run through the middle of downtown, and the old freight-yard energy is part of the texture. What sets Roanoke apart from other mid-sized Southern cities is where it sits: in a bowl of the Blue Ridge, with mountains visible from nearly every street and the Appalachian Trail tracing the ridgeline above town.

Roanoke's whole proposition is mountains you can reach from a downtown sidewalk. McAfee Knob, the most photographed viewpoint on the entire Appalachian Trail, is fifteen minutes northwest. Carvins Cove, the second-largest municipal park in the country, spreads 12,700 acres and more than 60 miles of trail just eight miles out. The 14-mile Roanoke River Greenway runs straight through the city, and Mill Mountain rises right at the edge of downtown, crowned by the illuminated Roanoke Star that gives the place its nickname, the Star City of the South. Few cities this affordable put this much genuine outdoor access this close.

The surprise is how much city comes with the mountains. Downtown has the free Taubman Museum of Art in a striking Randall Stout building, one of the oldest continuously operating farmers markets in the country, and a restaurant scene that has quietly gotten serious. Grandin Village, a walkable historic strip on the west side, keeps a 1932 movie palace, a food co-op, and the kind of independent shops that have vanished elsewhere. Anchoring it all is Carilion Clinic, the region's dominant health system and its largest employer, whose flagship hospital and Virginia Tech medical school give a valley city the specialist depth of somewhere much bigger.

On the Star as civic identity

The Roanoke Star has stood on Mill Mountain since 1949, nearly 90 feet of neon lit every night above the valley. It was built as a holiday retail promotion and simply never came down. Now it is the thing people mean when they say the Star City, a symbol you can see from most of town and hike to on your own two feet.

Where to live

Four ways to land in Roanoke.

The citywide typical home value is about $285K, but the range across the valley is wide, from a premier tree-lined district into the high five figures to attainable suburbs well under the typical figure. The four below deliberately span that range: walkable-premium, walkable-arts, small-town-independent, and suburban value. Pricing reflects 2026 estimates and varies by street, lot, and condition.

South Roanoke
Established · Walkable · Premier
The city's most prestigious neighborhood, quiet tree-lined streets climbing toward Mill Mountain, with direct greenway access and the Carilion campus close by. Popular with retirees who want established character and walkability to the parkway and river. The top of the market inside the city limits. Median: roughly $580K, with the largest historic homes higher.
Grandin Village / Raleigh Court
Walkable · Arts · Historic
The west side's walkable heart, built around the 1932 Grandin Theatre, a food co-op, and a strip of independent shops and restaurants. Early-20th-century homes on a grid, an easy bike or greenway ride to downtown. The genuine walkable-charm pick at a mid-market price. Range: roughly $265K–$300K.
Salem
Small-town · Safe · Independent
An independent city adjoining Roanoke to the west, with its own walkable downtown, low crime, and a strong community feel. Longwood Park and the Roanoke College area anchor it, and prices sit near the regional typical. The quiet-town option for retirees who want a real Main Street without the city proper. Median: roughly $270K.
Cave Spring / Vinton
Suburban · Value · Space
The value corners of the valley. Cave Spring, southwest in Roanoke County, offers newer suburban homes and yard space; Vinton, an eastern town with its own walkable downtown, runs a touch lower still. Both trade walkability for more house per dollar and easy trail access. Range: roughly $250K–$315K.
Healthcare: deeper than the population suggests

A regional hospital that anchors all of Southwestern Virginia.

🏥
Carilion Roanoke Memorial Hospital · Virginia Tech Carilion School of Medicine
The region's only Level I trauma center, and U.S. News & World Report's Best Regional Hospital in Southwestern Virginia, ranked among the top hospitals in the state and rated high performing in 15 procedures and conditions including cancer surgery, heart failure, cardiology, and geriatric-relevant care. At roughly 700 beds, it is the flagship of Carilion Clinic, the region's dominant health system, and the primary teaching hospital for the adjacent Virginia Tech Carilion School of Medicine. For a valley city, that academic affiliation buys real specialist depth close to home, with the University of Virginia about two hours northeast for the rare case that needs a top-tier academic center.

Visit Before You Decide

Stand at the overlook on Mill Mountain and the whole pitch is right in front of you: a real downtown sitting in a bowl of the Blue Ridge, with McAfee Knob a fifteen-minute drive off to the northwest. The photos of Roanoke sell the mountains, and the mountains are genuinely that good. What a visit tells you is the rest, whether a small airport and a quiet arts calendar feel like the right trade for waking up under those ridges every day.

Spend your nights where retirees actually settle. South Roanoke for tree-lined walkability, Grandin Village for the arts-and-co-op west side, Salem for a small-town Main Street, or Cave Spring for suburban space. Drive from a rental to Carilion Roanoke Memorial in weekday traffic, walk the greenway at dusk, sit through a Saturday at the City Market, and see whether the valley feels like home in February, not just on a bluebird October hike. Test the daily routine, not the highlight reel.

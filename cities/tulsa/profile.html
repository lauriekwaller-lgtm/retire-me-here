Should you actually move here?

Is Tulsa for you?

Tulsa is a value city carrying culture and medicine you would expect from a much larger one, and the reason is oil money that got spent on buildings instead of taken elsewhere. The catch is that Tulsa asks you to choose your neighborhood carefully, keep a car, and live with weather that has a season of its own. The retirees who are happy here decided all three were worth it.

✓
You'll love it here if…
  • The money has to stretch. A $194,000 citywide typical home value, a cost of living index near 85, and a realistic all-in retiree budget of $4,200 to $5,300 a month. Property tax runs about 0.79% effective, roughly $2,370 a year on a $300,000 house.
  • You want a real arts city at a small city's price. The Bob Dylan Center opened in 2022 with an archive of more than 100,000 items, a block from the Woody Guthrie Center. Philbrook is a 1920s Italian Renaissance villa full of art with formal gardens behind it. Cain's Ballroom has been booking bands since the Bob Wills era.
  • Healthcare depth matters more than scenery. Saint Francis Hospital was named the best hospital in Oklahoma in the 2025–26 U.S. News rankings, rated high performing in 19 procedures and conditions across a 1,112-bed campus. Ascension St. John runs the only Level I trauma center in northeastern Oklahoma.
  • Retirement income taxes are on your list. Oklahoma exempts Social Security entirely, adds a $10,000 per-person deduction on other retirement income at 65 and up, exempts military retirement pay, and levies no estate or inheritance tax. A senior valuation freeze can lock your assessed home value once you qualify.
✗
Skip Tulsa if…
  • Tornado Alley is a deal-breaker. This is the heart of severe weather country: tornadoes, giant hail, ice storms, straight-line wind. Homeowners insurance runs about $5,010 a year on a $300,000 dwelling at the Oklahoma average, well above the national norm, and that premium is part of the true price of the cheap house.
  • Hot, humid summers wear on you. July highs average around 93°F and August is worse. From June into September, outdoor life moves to early morning, evening, or indoors entirely.
  • You want mountains or coast. Tulsa is plains. Turkey Mountain Urban Wilderness and the River Parks trails are genuine, and the Gathering Place is exceptional, but there is no range to climb and no long-distance trail network to disappear into.
  • You won't shop by neighborhood. Citywide crime statistics sit near the 19th percentile nationally, driven by parts of the city no retiree is looking at. Brookside, Maple Ridge, and South Tulsa run 60th to 80th percentile; Bixby and Jenks run 80th to 95th. Where you buy is the whole difference, and it is not a decision you can make from a listing site.
The character of the place

Oil money, spent on culture.

Tulsa called itself the Oil Capital of the World, and for about forty years it was. The boom peaked in the 1920s, which is why downtown is one of the country's better collections of Art Deco: the Philtower, the Philcade, and Boston Avenue Methodist Church, a National Historic Landmark and one of the finest Art Deco churches anywhere. Then the industry consolidated to Houston and Tulsa stopped growing. What it did not do was stop building.

The philanthropy is the thing that never left. Waite Phillips handed the city his Italian Renaissance villa in 1938 and it became Philbrook. Nine decades later the George Kaiser Family Foundation put $465 million into the Gathering Place, a riverfront park that USA Today readers voted the best city park in the country, brought Bob Dylan's personal archive to Tulsa in 2016, and opened the Bob Dylan Center in 2022 a block from the Woody Guthrie Center. A $141 million Gilcrease Museum is going up on the hill northwest of downtown, due to reopen in spring 2027. For a city of roughly 410,000 people, that is a strange and generous amount of infrastructure.

The result is leverage. The neighborhoods retirees actually buy in run $300,000 to $500,000, which is real money in Oklahoma and a rounding error against the coasts, and they sit ten to twenty minutes from a top-ranked hospital and a world-class archive. The tradeoffs are not hidden: it is flat, it is hot in summer, it is a driving city, and the weather radio is not decoration. But the arithmetic on the other side is unusually good, and 2026 is a loud year to see it, with Route 66 turning 100 on November 11 and Tulsa, which trademarked the phrase Capital of Route 66, celebrating all year.

Where to live
Reading the numbers here: the $194K figure at the top is the Tulsa city-limits typical home value, and it averages in parts of north and west Tulsa that no retiree is shopping. The same averaging drives the citywide crime statistics. The four areas below are where retirees actually buy. They run from $300K to seven figures, and they sit between the 60th and 95th percentile nationally on safety.

Four Tulsas, depending on you.

The spread here runs from walkable urban village to top-rated south-metro suburb, with hospital access as the constant: Saint Francis sits at 61st and Yale, within fifteen minutes of all four. Pricing reflects February 2026 estimates and varies by block and lot.

Brookside
Walkable · Tree-lined · Cultural
The walkable pick. A mile-long strip of independent shops and restaurants along Peoria just south of midtown, with bungalows and renovated mid-century houses on the streets behind it. Close to Cherry Street and the Gathering Place, low crime for an in-city neighborhood, and the one place in Tulsa where a car-light week is plausible. Median: roughly $350K–$450K.
Maple Ridge
Historic · Premium · Central
Tulsa's mansion district, built when the oil money was new and still the most prestigious address in the city. Big historic houses, mature trees, strong neighborhood associations, and a walk to Cherry Street or downtown. The most house-per-dollar drama in the metro, and the widest price band. Median: roughly $500K, with the top blocks well past $1M.
South Tulsa
Suburban · Healthcare-proximate · Quiet
Established suburban Tulsa around 71st and Memorial, minutes from Saint Francis and the Woodland Hills shopping corridor. Conventional, quiet, easy, and the practical choice if proximity to specialists is the organizing principle of the move. Median: roughly $300K–$450K.
Bixby & Jenks
South metro · Top-rated · Very safe
Two south-metro suburbs about fifteen to twenty minutes from downtown, ranked the top two suburbs in the Tulsa area by Niche. Newer construction, more yard, and the strongest safety numbers in the metro at the 80th to 95th percentile. Median: roughly $300K–$425K.
Healthcare: the quiet reason this works

The state's top-ranked hospital, fifteen minutes from where retirees buy.

🏥
Saint Francis Hospital · Saint Francis Health System
Named the best hospital in Oklahoma in the 2025–26 U.S. News & World Report Best Hospitals rankings, rated high performing in 19 procedures and conditions, and recognized as a Best Regional Hospital for community access. At 1,112 beds it is the largest hospital in Oklahoma and the 11th largest in the nation, anchored by a 168-bed heart hospital and the region's leading trauma and emergency center. The health system was named a Top 15 Health System in 2025 in the Premier study published by Fortune, its second consecutive year. Ascension St. John Medical Center is the second-ranked hospital in the state and operates the only verified Level I trauma center in northeastern Oklahoma. For a metro of this size, that is more depth than the map suggests, and it is the reason South Tulsa reads differently to a buyer over 65 than it does to anyone else.

Visit Before You Decide

Tulsa owns the trademark on the phrase Capital of Route 66, and in 2026 the road turns 100, which makes this an unusually good year to be here. What you find when the parade passes is a city with Bob Dylan's personal archive, an Art Deco downtown, a $465 million riverfront park, and houses that cost a third of what the same life costs on a coast. The parts a brochure will not settle are the summer heat, the driving, and how different one Tulsa neighborhood feels from the next.

Pick your base carefully, because in Tulsa the base is the decision. Brookside and Maple Ridge give you the walkable, tree-lined version; South Tulsa and Jenks give you the quiet suburban one with newer construction and the strongest safety numbers. Drive from whichever you choose to Saint Francis at 61st and Yale at eight in the morning, buy groceries where you would actually buy them, and sit outside in the afternoon to see what the heat is really like. Test the daily routine, not the highlight reel.

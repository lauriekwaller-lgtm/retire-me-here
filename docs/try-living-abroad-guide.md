# Try Living Abroad Guide

Oct 20, 2026 · @Laurie Waller

Start with how long you want to stay, because that one rule decides which countries are open to you without a residence visa. Budget and place come second.

## Step 1: Pick your length of stay

For one place, 6 months is the practical ceiling in Latin America and 12 months is possible only in Australia. Anything longer in one country means a residence visa and its income test.

### Up to 3 months

- **Where:** almost anywhere, including Europe and Australia (free ETA)
- **Rule:** Europe allows 90 days in any 180, counted across all Schengen countries together
- **Watch:** Portugal, Spain, Greece and France share one 90-day clock

### Up to 6 months: Mexico, Panama, Costa Rica

- **Rule:** up to 180 days on arrival
- **Watch:** Mexico and Costa Rica can grant fewer days. Panama enforces strictly and usually wants 30 days out before you return

### Up to 6 months: Colombia

- **Rule:** 90 days, extendable to 180
- **Watch:** apply for the extension before day 90

### Up to 12 months: Australia

- **Rule:** a visitor visa (subclass 600) can be granted for 3, 6 or 12 months
- **Watch:** visa fee, high living costs, long flights. A trial only, not a path to stay

### Short stays only: Thailand

- **Rule:** 30 days since Sept 15, 2026, plus one 30-day extension
- **Watch:** just cut from 60 days; rules are still moving

**Rule of thumb:** for a first trial, 3 months is enough to know whether daily life suits you, and it works almost everywhere.

## Step 2: String stays together for a longer trial

You can spend most of a year abroad legally by moving between regions, because each region keeps its own clock. Back-to-back re-entries into the same country are what draw questions at the border.

- **Winter in Latin America, spring in Europe.** About 5 months in Mexico or Costa Rica, then up to 90 days in Portugal or Spain.
- **Europe in two halves.** 90 days in Europe, then 90 days outside Schengen (Latin America, or back home), then Europe again.
- **One long Australia stay.** A 12-month visitor visa covers the year in a single place, if the cost works.

Keep a simple day log with entry and exit dates for every country. Europe's limit counts days across all Schengen countries together.

## Step 3: Cover health and housing

Health coverage is the one step you can't skip. Original Medicare generally doesn't pay for care outside the US, and Medigap foreign emergency benefits carry a $50,000 lifetime cap.

- **Health:** buy travel medical or international health insurance that covers the full trial, including medical evacuation. Check age limits and pre-existing condition rules before you buy.
- **Housing:** book a furnished monthly rental for the first month only. Choose the neighborhood in person, then rent longer locally, usually for less.
- **Budget test:** live on the monthly budget you'd have if you moved for real. That's the point of the trial.

## Step 4: Before you go

- [ ] Choose length and country, then confirm the current entry rule on the State Department country page
- [ ] Check passport validity runs well past your return date
- [ ] Buy health insurance for the full stay, with evacuation cover
- [ ] Book the first month of a furnished rental
- [ ] Book onward or return travel; several countries ask for proof at check-in
- [ ] Get a card with no foreign transaction fees and a way to move money cheaply
- [ ] Fill prescriptions for the full stay, or confirm you can refill there
- [ ] Set up mail forwarding or a digital mail service
- [ ] Start a day log of entries and exits
- [ ] Tell your bank and card issuers your travel dates

QUESTIONS

The fine print, in plain English.

What is the difference between an estate tax and an inheritance tax?
An estate tax is paid by the estate itself before anything is distributed, and it only applies above an exemption threshold, often several million dollars. An inheritance tax is paid by the person who receives the money, is set by their relationship to the deceased, and in some states applies from the first dollar. A state can have one, both, or neither, which is why the filter treats them as two separate boxes.
What does Partial mean next to Social Security or retirement income?
Partial means the state taxes that income for some people and not others, usually depending on age or total income. The note on each state card spells out the mechanism, such as an income threshold below which Social Security is exempt, or a deduction that shelters the first slice of pension and IRA withdrawals. If you check a filter box, Partial states are excluded, because the box means the state does not tax it for anyone.
Where does this data come from, and how current is it?
Rates and structures come from the Tax Foundation's published tables, Social Security and retirement income treatment from Kiplinger and AARP's state guides, and death taxes from current legal summaries, all reconciled against enacted state law. Every state card shows the tax year the facts describe. The figures are summaries for typical retirees, not tax advice: confirm the details with the state before you move.
